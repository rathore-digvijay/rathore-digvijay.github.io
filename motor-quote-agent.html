Motor Quote Agent: a conversational path from question to issued policy

The first production LLM agent at InsuranceDekho. Partner agents describe what they need in plain language; the agent generates quotes, compares plans, looks up commission grids and issues policies across six-plus insurers.

Context and my role

Motor quoting on the partner platform was a form-driven journey: pick a make, model and variant from long dropdowns, then step through insurer-by-insurer comparison. Agents on a call with a customer wanted to ask one question and get an answer.

There was no applied-AI function in the organization when we started. I defined the charter — what we would build, what we would not, how we would measure it — and built AI Labs from the ground up with a team of four. I owned the architecture, the model and retrieval choices, the evaluation approach, and the case for shipping an agent into a regulated, money-moving flow.

Decisions and trade-offs

Hybrid retrieval instead of pure vector search

Make-Model-Variant resolution is the hinge of the whole journey: get the variant wrong and the premium is wrong. Embeddings alone confused near-identical variant strings, and keyword search alone missed colloquial phrasing. We run BM25 and vector similarity together over PostgreSQL with pgvector and fuse with RRF, with HNSW and IVFFlat indexing and a dedicated embedding ingestion pipeline. Costs one extra query path; buys accuracy where accuracy is money.

Tiered model routing

Intent classification and entity extraction run on a lightweight model; comparison and explanation run on a stronger one. That split is most of the reason average LLM cost per conversation lands around two cents and time-to-first-token stays sub-second.

A stateful graph, not a prompt chain

Quoting is a multi-turn negotiation: partial vehicle details, corrections, add-on changes, insurer switches. LangGraph gives explicit state and resumable steps, so a correction on turn nine does not restart the journey. Deterministic business rules — payout grids, eligibility, issuance — stay in normal services behind function calls rather than in the model.

Tracing and evals from day one

Every conversation is traced in Langfuse with per-step latency and cost, and regression evals gate prompt and model changes. Without that, nobody would have signed off on an agent touching policy issuance.

Team and how I led it

Four engineers, none of whom had shipped an LLM system before. We split the work into retrieval, orchestration and integrations, and ran a weekly design review where each owner defended their interface rather than their code. I kept the insurer integration contracts stable so the agent work could move fast without breaking the platform teams around us.

The harder leadership work was outside the team: agreeing with Product and Business on which parts of a regulated journey an agent may decide, and which parts it may only execute.
