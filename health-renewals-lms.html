Health Renewals LMS and voicebot: a calling floor that runs itself

A lead management system for 200+ calling agents, with voicebot automation handling around 500 outbound renewal calls a day. Outreach cost fell 30–40% and manual effort about 30%.

Architecture, delivery lead, ops stakeholder
Stack

Users
200+ calling agents and their team leads

Context and my role

Health renewals need a conversation. Sum insured changes, family members are added, the customer wants to compare before paying — so a large share of health renewal leads goes to a calling floor rather than a self-serve link. That floor was running on spreadsheets and manual allocation, with no reliable view of which leads had actually been worked.

I designed and led the build of the LMS that replaced it, and the voicebot layer in front of it. Much of my time went into sitting with the ops team to learn the real dispositions they use, rather than the ones a system designer would invent.

Decisions and trade-offs

Voicebot for the first touch, humans for intent

We did not try to automate the renewal conversation. The voicebot handles reminder and reachability calls and only routes interested customers to an agent, so the floor spends its day on leads that can convert. That kept the accuracy bar achievable while still cutting outreach cost 30–40%.

Automated allocation with an override

Leads are assigned by rule — expiry proximity, agent capacity, language, past ownership — but team leads can reassign. Fully automatic allocation looked cleaner and was rejected in review by the people who would have to live with it during a shift crunch.

Retry belongs in the dashboard

Every failure — communication, payment, booking — surfaces in the React ops dashboard with a retry action, so ops resolves most incidents without an engineer. That is the single change that most reduced our on-call noise.

Dispositions as a first-class model

The disposition taxonomy is versioned and reportable rather than free text, which is what makes agent performance and funnel reporting comparable month to month.

Team and how I led it

Backend and frontend engineers from my group, delivered against an ops timeline rather than an engineering one. I ran this as a joint project with the operations leadership: weekly demos on the floor, dispositions and reports reviewed by the people using them, and a phased rollout by team so we could correct the allocation rules before the whole floor depended on them.
